Topic 1: What Does Expanding Mean?

Learn

Expanding means removing brackets by multiplying what is outside the bracket by every term inside.

For example, 3(x + 2) means 3 × (x + 2). The 3 must multiply both x and 2.

Do not multiply only the first term.

Worked example

3(x + 2)

3 × x = 3x

3 × 2 = 6

Answer: 3x + 6

Topic 2: Expanding a Positive Bracket

Learn

When a positive number or term is outside a bracket, multiply it by every term inside.

Keep the signs inside the bracket.

Write each multiplication step before combining anything.

Worked example

3(2x + 5)

3 × 2x = 6x

3 × 5 = 15

Answer: 6x + 15

Topic 3: Expanding a Negative Bracket

Learn

A negative outside the bracket changes the signs of the terms when it is multiplied through.

Think of −1 multiplying every term inside.

Example: −(x + 4) = −x − 4.

Worked example — slowly

−2(x + 3)

Step 1: Multiply −2 by the first term:
−2 × x = −2x

Step 2: Multiply −2 by the second term:
−2 × 3 = −6

Remember: − × + = −

Answer: −2x − 6

Topic 4: Expanding When the Bracket Has a Minus

Learn

The minus sign inside the bracket is part of the term.

Multiply the outside number by both terms.

Example: 3(x − 2) = 3x − 6.

Worked example — slowly

4(2x − 3)

Step 1: Multiply 4 by 2x:
4 × 2x = 8x

Step 2: The second term is −3, not +3.
4 × (−3) = −12

Remember: + × − = −

Answer: 8x − 12

Topic 5: Expanding and Then Combining Like Terms

Learn

Sometimes expansion creates like terms that can be combined.

First multiply through the bracket. Then look for like terms.

Do not combine unlike terms.

Worked example

2(x + 3) + x

Expand first: 2x + 6 + x

Combine 2x + x = 3x

Answer: 3x + 6

Topic 6: Expanding and Simplifying

Learn

For a longer expression, work in stages.

Step 1: expand every bracket.

Step 2: remove the brackets.

Step 3: combine like terms.

Step 4: check that unlike terms are still separate.

Worked example — never skip the signs

4(y + 2) − 2(y − 1)

Step 1: Expand the first bracket:
4 × y = 4y
4 × 2 = +8

So the first bracket becomes 4y + 8.

Step 2: Expand the second bracket. The outside number is −2.

−2 × y = −2y

−2 × (−1) = +2

Why +2? Because − × − = +.

So the second bracket becomes −2y + 2.

Step 3: Put everything together:
4y + 8 − 2y + 2

Step 4: Combine like terms:
4y − 2y = 2y
8 + 2 = 10

Final answer: 2y + 10
